OEM and ODM

Solution OEM and ODM for wayfinding projects

01

Definition

What does solution OEM and ODM mean on a wayfinding project?

OEM means the integrator presents the finished system to the end client under its own name, with the platform licensed behind the scenes. ODM goes further: the specification, the hardware selection, the interface configuration and the documentation are produced for the integrator to ship, so the integrator can win the tender without owning a mapping team or a navigation codebase.

In practice most projects sit between the two. The integrator holds the client relationship, the installation, the cabling and the first line support; we hold the map production, the positioning design, the software and the hardware sourcing where the integrator wants one supplier and one warranty chain. A CCTV or structured cabling contractor taking on wayfinding for the first time usually wants the ODM end, because the tender needs a specification written in a language the consultant will accept.

Nothing in the package requires the integrator to disclose us to the client, though we do not object if they do. Non disclosure is the default, and the project manager on our side works to the integrator's reporting rhythm, attends design team meetings when invited and issues progress in the format the integrator already uses with its client. Where a joint technical session with the consultant is needed, we attend as part of the integrator's team rather than as a second supplier in the room.

02

Pre tender

How is the specification written?

We start from the tender documents the consultant has issued and write the wayfinding section to match their format: performance requirement, scope of supply, technical specification, submittal list, testing and commissioning, training and warranty. Where the consultant has written something the site cannot support, we say so before the bid is submitted rather than after award, because a kiosk count that assumes a position with no power is a variation waiting to happen.

The performance requirement is written so it can be tested. Positioning accuracy is stated as typical with the technology named and the site dependency declared. Route correctness is stated as reachability of every published point of interest from every terminal with the accessible alternative available. Screen readability is stated against viewing distance and measured lux. A specification that can be tested protects the integrator at practical completion, when the consultant asks for evidence.

Where the integrator wins on a design and build basis, the same document becomes the basis of the submittal pack, with the map production schedule, the position plan and the interface configuration attached. We also supply the short form compliance statement that goes into the bid return, listing the clauses we meet, the clauses we meet by equivalent performance and the few we do not offer, so nobody discovers a gap during the technical evaluation.

  • Section written to the consultant's tender format
  • Performance requirements that can be measured at commissioning
  • Scope of supply separated from scope of interface
  • Submittal schedule with drawing and document list
  • Variation risks flagged before the bid goes in
  • Design and build version for single stage tenders

03

Supply

What hardware do you source?

The bill of materials covers three groups: terminals, positioning infrastructure and network equipment. Terminals are directory kiosks in the form factors the site needs, with panel size, brightness and enclosure rated to the location. Positioning infrastructure is BLE gateways, UWB anchors or the site's own Wi-Fi access points used for fingerprinting or RTT, specified after a site walk rather than from a density formula alone. Network equipment is the switches, injectors and small routers that keep the terminals reachable when the landlord will not provide a data point.

We consolidate the hardware onto one purchase with one delivery schedule and one warranty chain where the integrator wants that, or supply software and specification only where the integrator has a preferred hardware partner. Beacons and anchors are specified and integrated, not manufactured by us, and the accuracy figures we quote are typical values for the technology in a real building, confirmed in the calibration phase.

Every line in the bill of materials carries a location, a quantity, a mounting method and a spare allowance, because the integrator needs that detail to price labor and the consultant needs it to check compliance. The bill is issued in the integrator's own template where one exists, with our part numbers mapped to the format their procurement system expects, so the purchase order can be raised without re keying the schedule.

  • Directory kiosks across 32 to 55 inch with enclosure options
  • BLE gateways, UWB anchors or the site's own access points
  • Switches, injectors and cellular routers for terminal links
  • Mounting kits, fixings and cable routes per position
  • Spare unit allowance stated per hardware category
  • Single warranty chain where consolidated supply is chosen

04

License

How is the software licensed?

Licensing is per deployment, not per seat, and it is scoped to a named venue with a defined terminal count and a support term. A deployment license covers the platform, the terminal server for up to 100 kiosks, the map editor, the CMS, the SDK and the API for that venue. Multi-venue portfolios take a volume structure with a parent account for reporting and per venue branding.

Hosting is chosen per project. SaaS is the default and the cheapest to start. Private cloud suits an integrator who needs a dedicated instance with its own backup window. On premise suits government, healthcare and defense adjacent projects where the client will not allow venue data to leave the building, and it comes as a release package with an update path rather than a live managed service.

The license does not transfer to a different client without a new registration, which protects the integrator's account. Upgrades are included in the support term and are applied to the shared engine, so a venue does not end up on a version that nobody maintains. The version each venue runs is recorded in the handover pack, and a rollback path is kept for one previous release in case an update conflicts with an integration on site.

  • Per deployment license scoped to one venue
  • Terminal server included for up to 100 kiosks
  • Map editor, CMS, SDK and API in the base license
  • Volume structure for portfolio and multi-client partners
  • SaaS, private cloud or on premise hosting
  • Upgrades included during the support term

05

Program

What is the delivery schedule?

The reference schedule runs from survey to handover in 6 to 10 weeks for a single venue, phased so the integrator can show progress on site. Survey takes 3 to 5 days on a 50,000 to 100,000 m2 building, and its output is the position plan, the positioning design and the hardware bill. Map production takes 2 to 3 weeks and runs in parallel with platform setup and integration at 2 to 4 weeks.

Kiosk lead time runs alongside map work and is the item that most often moves a date, so it is quoted with a buffer and tracked from the day the purchase order is confirmed. On-site calibration and training take 1 week, and handover follows when the acceptance tests in the specification have been run and recorded. The schedule is typical and confirmed after survey, and we re baseline it at design freeze rather than at award.

  • Week 1: survey and position plan
  • Weeks 1 to 3: map production in parallel
  • Weeks 2 to 5: platform setup and integration
  • Weeks 2 to 6: kiosk lead time and delivery
  • Week 6 or later: calibration, training, acceptance
  • Rebaseline at design freeze, weekly progress report

06

Handover pack

What documentation and training come with the delivery?

The handover pack is written for the integrator's engineer and the client's facilities team, not for our own staff. It holds the as built position plan with mounting heights and cable routes, the hardware schedule with serial numbers, the network requirements including the endpoints each terminal calls, the operator guide for the map editor and the CMS, the acceptance test record and the warranty terms with the claim route.

Training runs in two tracks. The technical track covers the platform, the map editor, positioning calibration and the API for the integrator's engineers, and it is delivered before handover so the integrator can run the acceptance test unaided. The operator track covers tenant data, campaigns and terminal health for the client team, and it is short enough to be repeated when staff change.

Documents are supplied in editable form so the integrator can re brand them, which matters when the pack goes to a client under the integrator's name. Drawings go out as CAD and PDF, guides as word processor files, and the acceptance test record as a printable form with the pass criteria already written in. We review one branded copy with the integrator before first issue so the naming, the logo placement and the contact block are right.

  • As built position plan with mounting heights and routes
  • Hardware schedule with serial numbers and locations
  • Network requirements and endpoint list
  • Operator guides for map editor and CMS
  • Acceptance test record signed at commissioning
  • Technical and operator training tracks, editable documents

07

After sales

How do warranty and spare parts work?

The standard term is 12 months on supplied hardware, with software updates included during the support term and extended support available for a venue that expects to run the system for a decade. Claims go through one route: the integrator logs the fault, we triage against the hardware schedule and the terminal diagnostics, and a replacement ships against the agreed spares allowance rather than waiting for the failed unit to come back to the factory.

The spare parts policy is set at order, not at failure. A venue with 16 kiosks usually holds one spare player module and one spare panel of the dominant size, and a beacon deployment holds a spare count of around two to three percent of the installed base. Those numbers are stated in the handover pack so the client can budget for them, and the spares are interchangeable across the estate, which keeps the stock small.

Consumables are minimal on a kiosk: a cleaning routine, a filter where the enclosure has one, and a printed slip roll on units with a printer. Everything else is a module swap, and the modules are documented so a facilities technician can perform the swap with a key and a screwdriver. A player module swap takes a few minutes, and the failed unit returns to the integrator's stores for a bench check rather than being scrapped on site.

  • 12 months standard term on supplied hardware
  • Software updates included during the support term
  • One claim route from the integrator into our desk
  • Spare player module and dominant panel size per venue
  • Two to three percent beacon spare allowance
  • Module swap documented for facilities technicians

08

Close out

How is a project handed over?

Handover is a defined event, not a date on a program. It happens when the acceptance tests in the specification have been run and recorded, the handover pack is delivered, the training attendance is signed and the support route is live with named contacts on both sides. Anything outstanding is listed with an owner and a date, and the list is agreed in writing before the site team leaves.

After handover the integrator holds first line support and the client relationship, and we hold second line for engine behavior, map corrections and platform defects. A defects liability period runs against the recorded acceptance, and map content changes after handover are either handled by the integrator's trained operator or by us under a support line item. Escalation paths, response targets and named contacts are printed on one page in the handover pack, because that page is the one that gets pinned up in the control room.

For multi site rollouts the same handover is repeated per venue with the parent account already in place, which is why the second venue usually takes less time than the first. The parent account carries the reporting view across the set, the per venue branding and the license registrations, so adding a venue is an administrative step rather than a new platform project. A quarterly review keeps the client asset register, the spare stock and the version record in step.

FAQ

Common questions

Ask a specific question

Can we bid a wayfinding package without naming your company?

Yes. OEM delivery is non disclosure by default: the specification, the documentation and the submittals are issued in the integrator's name, and the client sees the integrator as the system supplier. We work to the integrator's reporting rhythm and do not contact the end client unless the integrator asks us to attend a technical review.

What if the consultant already specified another platform?

We read the specified scope and either match it or tell you where it cannot be met. Where the specification names a proprietary item, the common route is a compliance statement with an equivalent performance offer, which the consultant accepts or rejects on the technical merit rather than on the brand. We write that comparison for you.

Is there a minimum project size?

The order minimum is one venue, so a single building is viable. What changes with size is the economics: a 6 to 10 week schedule carries fixed survey and map production effort, so a 4,000 m2 clinic and a 120,000 m2 mall take similar calendar time in the early phases even though the scope differs by an order of magnitude.

Who handles the cabling and installation?

The integrator's own teams or their subcontractors, in most projects. We supply the position plan, the power and network requirements, the mounting kits and the commissioning procedure, and we attend the calibration. Where the integrator has no site capability, we can provide a supervised installation scope, quoted separately.

How do you handle a change of client after award?

A license is registered to a named client and venue. If the project transfers to a different end client, the registration is moved with a short amendment, and the account protection then applies to the new owner. The integrator keeps the relationship and we do not treat the transfer as a new sale.

What happens when a kiosk fails in year two?

The integrator logs the fault against the hardware schedule, the terminal diagnostics are pulled remotely, and a module ships from the spares allowance if the fault is hardware. If the fault is content or network, it is usually resolved remotely. Out of warranty, modules are supplied as spare parts at the agreed schedule price.

Can we combine OEM delivery with a white label brand?

Yes, and it is common. The OEM package covers specification, hardware, licensing and handover; the white label layer puts the integrator's brand on the app, portal, kiosk skin and documents. The two are configured together, so the client sees one product with one identity throughout the delivery and after it.
