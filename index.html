    Positioning route

    Three ways to know where someone stands

    Accuracy claims are easy; floor detection and stale maps are what break a route. We specify the cheapest technology that satisfies your accuracy target on the floors you actually have.

    Positioning options and what they cost you in infrastructure
    RouteTypical accuracyInfrastructureFits
    BLE beacons 1 to 3 m Gateways on the main corridor, battery beacons Malls, hospitals, museums, most retrofits
    UWB 0.1 to 0.3 m Anchors every 20 to 30 m, wired power Industrial, logistics, high-value assets
    Wi-Fi 3 to 8 m Existing access points, controller data access Airports, campuses with dense coverage
    Hybrid 1 to 2 m BLE plus Wi-Fi plus map matching Large multi-floor venues, phased budget

    Typical values for surveyed venues. Accuracy is confirmed during calibration, not promised in a proposal.

    Ask a specific question

    What is included in an indoor wayfinding solution?

    Five working parts: a mapped venue (floor plates, rooms, points of interest and a walk graph), a positioning layer that fixes the visitor on the map, a routing engine that builds a step-free or fastest path, the visitor surfaces (kiosk, app, signage) and the admin platform that keeps content, tenants and analytics in one place.

    How is an indoor wayfinding project priced?

    Cost follows four quantities: mapped area, number of floors, number of points of interest, and the number of surfaces and integrations. Positioning hardware and directory kiosks are the largest line items. We price per project after a short survey rather than quoting a rate card that does not survive contact with a real building.

    How long does deployment take for a large mall?

    A 100,000 m2 mall with six retail floors typically runs 6 to 10 weeks from survey to acceptance, phased so that the map and directory layer can go live before positioning calibration is finished. Schedules are confirmed after the survey, because floor plan quality is the single biggest variable.

    Can it use the Wi-Fi network we already run?

    Often yes, with conditions. Wi-Fi positioning needs a surveyed access point layout, consistent coverage and a controller that exposes signal data. Where coverage is patchy, a small number of BLE gateways along the main corridor usually fixes accuracy for a fraction of the cost of rebuilding the network.

    Do we need beacons, UWB or Wi-Fi?

    BLE beacons cover most venues at meter-level accuracy and low cost. UWB gives 10 to 30 cm for industrial or asset-adjacent use but needs denser infrastructure. Wi-Fi is cheapest where coverage is already good but is less accurate on floors. We specify and integrate the mix your building supports.

    How do you handle privacy?

    We collect the minimum needed to route a visitor: search terms, origin and destination zones, timestamps and an anonymised session. No camera identity tracking, no biometrics. Staff location is limited to consenting devices during a shift. Aggregated reporting is available without any person-level data leaving the venue.
