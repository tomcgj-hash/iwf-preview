Case 01

One level below grade · more than 30 themed street zones · AR guidance in production

A single-level underground retail complex in southern China

The problem

A large underground retail floor with themed street zones. There is no floor change to help a visitor build a mental model, the ceiling limits where hardware can be mounted, and the corridors look alike, so a 2D map alone does not stop people losing direction.

What was installed

  • 3D indoor map of the single level, with street zones as the primary wayfinding unit
  • Turn-by-turn navigation with landmark references tied to zone names rather than distances
  • AR camera guidance, enabled because the corridor views repeat and the map arrows do not resolve ambiguity
  • Reverse car finding so a visitor can return to a parked car from any zone
  • Zone-level heatmaps and a search log dashboard for the operating team

How the operator uses it

The operating team reads zone heat and search logs to see which streets get lost, and repositions directory screens and promotion content accordingly. Arcade-level tenant changes are handled in the map editor by the venue team, not by us.

This is the hardest shape we deploy: single level, no vertical cues, heavily themed. It is also why the site says AR is a fallback aid rather than a default.

Case 02

Multi-city chain · standardised deployment · map, directory, parking and patrol in one template

A national retail chain rolling the same system out across its malls

The problem

A chain that opens new complexes every year cannot treat wayfinding as a bespoke project per building. It needs one content model, one admin platform and a repeatable deployment so that each new site does not restart the design work.

What was installed

  • A standard map and POI template reused across sites, with tenant data imported per mall
  • Tenant directory and brand guide content, so a shopper can find a brand rather than a shop number
  • Parking guidance with reverse car finding integrated to each site parking system
  • Patrol check-in with routes and completion statistics for the operations team

How the operator uses it

The chain compares performance between sites because the content model is identical. Patrol completion and reverse-car sessions are reported per mall, and a new site is configured from the template rather than from scratch.

Standardisation is the real product here. A template that survives 30 different floor plans is harder than any single deployment.

Case 03

Mixed-use commercial · map, navigation, tenant directory and AR promotion

A commercial project inside a diversified industrial group

The problem

A group whose main business is not retail owns a commercial project. Its facilities team has no in-house wayfinding capability, and the marketing team wants the space to be usable for events and brand promotion, not only for finding shops.

What was installed

  • 3D indoor map with floor and tenant content maintained by the venue
  • Navigation from the car park and entrances to tenants, with a directory layer
  • Event and promotion content in the same system, so a campaign is a content update rather than a new app build
  • AR promotional content used during events, inside the same map rather than a separate application

How the operator uses it

The venue runs promotions through the same content system it uses for wayfinding. The facilities team owns the map, and events do not require a development cycle.

Most venue owners are not retailers. The system has to be operable by a facilities team with no mapping background.

FAQ

Questions about these deployments

Can't see your question? Send it with your floor count and we answer within one working day.

Ask a specific question

Why are the clients not named?

Because we do not have written permission to name them, and using a client logo without consent is the fastest way to lose a reference. The scope, the building type and what was installed are accurate; the names are withheld until the venue agrees otherwise.

Where are the outcome percentages?

Not published. The operators measured their own results, and we are not the party that verified them. If you want numbers, ask on a call: we will say which figures exist, who measured them and what we can and cannot share.

Can we speak to a reference?

Usually yes for a serious project. Reference calls are arranged with the venue directly, after they agree, and we do not coach what they say. It takes a few weeks to arrange, so ask early in the process.

Are these the only deployments?

No, these are the three that show different building shapes: a hard single-level underground site, a chain that needs standardisation, and a mixed-use project run by a non-retail group. The site carries only what we can describe accurately.

Can you deliver the same scope for our building?

The modules yes, the quantities no. Kiosk counts, gateway density and schedule follow your plans and the survey. The acceptance test is what turns a scope like these into a contract.
