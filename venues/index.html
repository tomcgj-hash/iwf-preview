Venues

The engine is the same. The content is not.

A mall routes by tenant and promotion, a hospital by department and appointment, an exhibition centre by hall and stand. Below is what actually changes between them, and what a scope needs from each.

Shared layer

Four things every deployment needs

Whatever the building, the same four layers decide whether the thing works after handover.

A current map

Floor plates, rooms, points of interest and the walkable graph, including which doors are actually open.

A position

BLE, UWB, Wi-Fi or hybrid, chosen for the building rather than the brochure, with floor detection that holds at stairwells.

A route engine

Turn-by-turn steps with landmarks, floor changes, and a step-free option that removes escalators and stairs.

A surface

Directory kiosk, venue app or signage that reads the same content, so one tenant change propagates everywhere.

01

mall

Shopping malls

40,000 to 120,000 m2, 3 to 6 retail levels, 1 to 3 parking levels

Tenant churn, promotions, parking, and a directory kiosk the operator has to keep current without calling us.

Measured afterwards: Searches per zone, route completion, reverse-car sessions, top destinations per floor.

02

hospital

Hospitals and clinics

Multi-building campuses, 5 to 15 buildings, outpatient and diagnostics

Department names change more often than buildings; accessibility is the default route, not an option; patient movement must be aggregated at collection.

Measured afterwards: Kiosk searches by department, accessibility route share, wrong-building arrivals reported at reception.

03

airport

Airports

Terminals of 200,000 m2 and above, landside and airside

Tight connections, transfers, late passengers, multi-terminal navigation, and signage that cannot keep pace with gate changes.

Measured afterwards: Gate searches, transfer route completions, kiosk use by journey stage.

04

campus

Campuses

10 to 40 buildings, wings and room numbering that no visitor can decode

Building, wing, floor and room references that must resolve to one destination; semester changes; open days.

Measured afterwards: Room searches, routes started from the portal, event-day load.

05

museums + shows

Museums and exhibition centres

Single building or hall-based venues with per-event layouts

Curated routes and temporary exhibitions; in exhibition centres the hall plan changes every event and must be republished in days.

Measured afterwards: Route sequence completion, stop dwell, per-event republish time.

Surfaces

Kiosks and screens are part of the map

A touch directory at the entrance, a tenant board beside the lift, a screen in the food court: they read the same points of interest as the phone. When a tenant moves, one edit updates all of them, which is the difference between a system and a set of screens.

  • Touch directory: search, floor selector, route handed to the phone by QR code
  • Wall and lift-lobby boards: floor directory, promotions, event schedule
  • Remote content management with a change reflected on every surface
  • Optional badge reader on a kiosk for staff check-in, with no identity capture beyond the badge

Operations

Four modules that reuse the same map

People counting

Footfall and zone occupancy from sensor counters or from search and route behaviour, reported per zone. Never from camera identity.

Indoor geofencing

Virtual zones that trigger when a device enters, leaves or dwells, used for restricted areas, staff zones and mustering.

Staff positioning

Dispatch, patrol and emergency response on consenting devices during a shift, with an access log and a retention limit.

Patrol check-in

Routes with checkpoints, missed-checkpoint alerts and completion statistics.

Ask a specific question

Can the same platform handle a mall and a hospital?

Yes, and the engine is identical: map, walk graph, positioning, routing. What changes is the content model. A mall is organised by tenant and promotion, a hospital by department and appointment. Mixing the two is a content problem, not a software problem.

Which venue type is the hardest?

Large single-level underground retail. There is no floor change to help a visitor build a mental model, the ceiling limits where gateways can go, and themed zones change tenants between seasons. Multi-level malls are more forgiving.

Do we need different hardware for an airport?

Usually less per square metre and more per interface. Airport coverage is often already dense enough for Wi-Fi based positioning, but the integration list is longer: flight information, transfer desks, accessibility services and multiple terminal systems.

How do exhibition centres republish a hall layout?

The hall shell stays mapped and the stand layout is imported per event, so the walk graph is updated rather than rebuilt. That is why an event republish can be a few days instead of a new project.

Can we start with one building and add others?

Yes. Multi-building campuses are common, and each building can go live separately against its own acceptance test. The admin platform manages them together once more than one is live.

What do you need to scope a venue?

Current floor plans, the number of levels including parking, an approximate tenant or department count, the systems you want connected, and the venue type. With those five items we can place the building against a reference configuration.

Are operations modules included?

They are optional modules on the same map. Most venues start with navigation and add people counting or patrol later, once the map and content are stable. Nothing is rebuilt when a module is added.
