What to know before you buy wayfinding

Written for the people who have to sign the acceptance test: facility managers, IT leads, procurement teams and integrators. No product pitch, and no numbers we cannot defend.

KNOWLEDGE 01 / 9 min

Indoor Navigation Guide: How It Works and What It Costs

Indoor navigation combines four layers: a multi-floor map, a positioning method, a routing engine and a presentation layer. Beacons, ultra-wideband or Wi-Fi produce the position, the walk graph produces the route, and the app, kiosk or AR view presents it. Cost follows area, floor count, points of interest and integrations rather than the screen.

KNOWLEDGE 02 / 8 min

Indoor Map Accuracy: What Precision Really Means

Indoor map accuracy is how closely drawn geometry matches the building. Positioning accuracy is how closely a reported position matches the visitor. A route fails when either number is wrong, or when the walk graph skips a door. Accept a floor only after measured checks on both, on the handsets visitors will use.

Read the guide
KNOWLEDGE 03 / 9 min

Wayfinding Project Cost: How Budgets Are Actually Built

A wayfinding budget is built from six blocks: survey and map production, positioning hardware, kiosks and displays, platform license, integration and customization, deployment and training, plus annual support. Area drives survey and mapping, floor count drives vertical work, points of interest drive tenant data, and integrations drive engineering hours.

Read the guide
KNOWLEDGE 04 / 8 min

Indoor navigation vs GPS: why satellites stop at the door

Satellite positioning reaches a phone in open sky and stops at the building envelope. Indoors you need a venue-installed layer, BLE at 1-3 m, UWB at 0.1-0.3 m or Wi-Fi at 3-8 m, with a multi-floor map and a routing engine. A satellite fix tells you where the building is, never where you are inside it.

Read the guide
KNOWLEDGE 05 / 8 min

Privacy in indoor positioning: what a venue signs up to

An indoor system collects search terms, routes, dwell times, plates and device or badge identifiers. Some is personal data; some is only personal while a key can re-link it. A venue needs a purpose, a retention period and an access rule per data type, staff location treated as employee monitoring, and a contract naming fields and deletion.
